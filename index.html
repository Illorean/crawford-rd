Strategic thesis

Four reasons the Crawford Rd play is timed correctly, and how to structure it.

01

The school district is being built next door

Lake Travis ISD is constructing a new high school and elementary school directly on Crawford Rd. For production buyers at this price band, "brand-new LTISD high school across the street" is the single highest-value selling point you can offer.

Adjacent listings — including 4519 Crawford and 4000 Crawford — explicitly reference the school in their MLS marketing. This isn't speculation; it's documented in the market.

02

DR Horton and Pulte have already underwritten this corridor

Pulte operates West Cypress Hills — 650 lots, 1.5 miles southwest, $490–650K. DR Horton runs Sola Vista within it. The thesis "production at this price band absorbs in Spicewood" is no longer a thesis — it's a fact pattern.

Your conversation isn't "will builders come here". It's "which builders, on what terms".

03

Multi-builder structure beats single-builder

Unlike Pace Bend, where Toll's brand carries the price story, at the $475–725K band you maximize absorption by splitting lots across three builders. The Travisso playbook: DR Horton on 50' lots, Pulte or CastleRock on 60', David Weekley or Tri Pointe on premium 70' lots.

This balances velocity (DR Horton volume) with pricing tension (Weekley premium) and reduces single-builder concentration risk.

04

The 129-acre option is where the real strategy lives

At 250 homes on 40 acres, density would be ~6.25 units/acre — far too tight for what LTISD buyers expect with hill country adjacency. The 129-acre expansion is the project.

If the 89-acre extension falls through, reframe: ~80 boutique-production homes at $550–750K on the original 40 acres. Different deal entirely — but still viable.
